Bring your questions about Earth’s past.

I am seeking graduate students interested in how rocks and landscapes record environmental change.

Potential projects connect coastal landscapes, sea-level history, carbonate chemistry, and quantitative methods. Your interests and preparation help shape the balance of field, laboratory, and computational work.

Directions we could explore together.

These questions offer starting points for a project. Related interests in stratigraphy, carbon-cycle history, and quantitative Earth science are also welcome.

Coastal landscapes and sea-level history.

When can reefs, beaches, dunes, and rock layers distinguish sea-level change from the redistribution of sediment?

Combine field mapping and photogrammetry with models of landscape evolution. Possible approaches include sediment transport, wave calculations, and machine learning that makes large sets of simulations practical.

Skills to develop: field interpretation, surveying, coding, numerical modeling, and statistical inference.

Carbonate chemistry and geological time.

How do grains and cements acquire and modify their isotope records, and when do those records constrain deposition?

Connect samples to field relationships, investigate their components with microscopy and isotope measurements, and test their histories with quantitative models.

Skills to develop: sampling, microscopy, analytical geochemistry, method development, and uncertainty analysis.

Independence with support.

Students lead their own research with close mentorship. My aim is to help you develop the judgment to choose useful questions, test explanations, and make scientific decisions with confidence.

Regular individual meetings and shared work-in-progress discussions support that development. The wider Earth History community at UVic brings our group together with those of Anne-Sofie Ahm and Jon Husson for weekly exchange across career stages.

What you can expect

  • Project scope and training adapted to your strengths and interests.
  • Explicit expectations for feedback, authorship, and a sustainable working rhythm.
  • Support for learning field, laboratory, and computational methods.
  • Discussion of equipment, research costs, and travel needs before work begins.
  • Training in reproducible data analysis, documented code, writing, and scientific communication.

Different starting points, shared curiosity.

You do not need to arrive with experience in every method. Preparation in geology, chemistry, physics, mathematics, computing, or related areas can contribute to these questions.

We welcome different backgrounds and perspectives and are committed to a positive, inclusive research environment. We can discuss project emphasis and ways to participate when field travel presents a barrier.

Degree pathways

I welcome inquiries about MSc and PhD research. One possible pathway begins in the MSc program, with transfer to a PhD after about a year when progress and program requirements support it. MSc completion and entry with an existing master’s degree are also possibilities.

Contact me to discuss project fit, timing, and available funding. Formal admissions requirements and procedures are set by UVic.
