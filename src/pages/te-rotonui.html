A closer look at Te Rotonui.

How do the shape, texture, and relationships of coastal rocks help us read their history?

Explore a surveyed outcrop on Mitiaro in the Southern Cook Islands. The field team visited this site, HS123, on 10 June 2026 as part of the HiSEAS campaign.

Describe the rock and its setting.

The photograph records a rough limestone surface and a recessed coastal wall above lower coastal rock. A wider view establishes how these features connect along the shore.

Field descriptions add information a model cannot supply by itself: grain types, fossil structures, contacts between deposits, and evidence of alteration.

What history could explain these features?

The present coastline combines deposition, later erosion, and changes in relative sea level. Some features may record the growth of the reef; others reflect what happened after the limestone formed.

A recess or terrace is a starting observation. Its origin and significance require geological context, rather than an elevation or shape alone.

Connect geometry, samples, and time.

Compare the digital outcrop with field photographs and mapped sample positions. Test whether fossils retain their original growth relationships, and investigate what their ages and preservation can establish.

Survey observations and water-level references are needed to relate heights to sea level. Models of land motion then help connect a local history to changes in ice sheets.
