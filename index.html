Reading Earth’s history in rocks and landscapes.

We investigate how sediments preserve—and sometimes obscure—changes in sea level, climate, and the carbon cycle.

Our group at the University of Victoria combines field geology, geochemistry, and quantitative models to test explanations for Earth’s past.

How do we read a changing planet?

Rocks are records shaped by the processes that built and altered them. Understanding those processes lets us ask better questions of the past.

What do ancient coastlines tell us about sea level and ice sheets?

Connect fossil reefs, beaches, and dunes across whole landscapes to test histories of sea-level change.

When did sediments form, and how did they become rock?

Investigate geological clocks and the chemical changes that accompany deposition, cementation, and alteration.

Distinguish environmental change from the effects of sediment transport, local chemistry, and uncertain timing.
